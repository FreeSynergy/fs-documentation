# Why FreeSynergy exists

**Draft (2026-11-06), written from what the project's founder has said; it will grow.**

> We don't write software. We write a manifesto that others can use too.

## Software for people

Software should serve people, not the other way round. So we do not describe what a tool can do; we ask what it is
*for*. Nobody wants a Kanban board. People want to know what to do next.

We want to be human. Small things matter too: an animation that makes someone smile can be the reason they stay.

## Free, for good

Data and communication must not depend on money. FreeSynergy is free forever and open to everyone, with **no
discrimination against the poor**. There will never be a paywall: spending time and energy on keeping someone away from
code makes no sense.

## What belongs together stays together

Money has shaped how software is cut up. What can be sold separately was built separately. Paint and Photoshop are
built as two programs, when the strong engine of Photoshop could carry a simple Paint too, because not everyone is a
Picasso.

In money terms that makes no sense. In human terms it does. So we cut by what really belongs together: **one strong
engine, and many frontends on top of it, from very simple to complete.** The engine may offer far more than a frontend
uses. A message is a message, whichever program brought it.

## Interfaces are targets

Every topic has one interface: messages, files, people, groups, databases. It is the same for every provider, so that
any provider can be swapped or added. That is why standards are absolutely necessary to us. See
[the interfaces](interfaces/index.md).

**An interface is a target, not something that grows.** A growing interface keeps changing, and growth has no end
point. A target has one. If an interface must change, that is a jump to a new version, which everyone can see coming.
People grow, and need no targets. Projects that work together loosely do, or nobody knows when something changes.

## Standards first, nothing copied

We analyse what programs do, and build it ourselves. Where a standard exists, we use it. Where none exists, we write one
and offer it to everyone. We look especially for what the open-source world does not have yet, and then bring it all
together.

We would rather support people with good new ideas than settle for what is old and slow. We use and support projects
such as Blinc, Turso, Kanidm, Zentinel and Web of Trust, and RustFS for storage.

## Your data, and you choose when it leaves

You work on a copy, and it is kept for you at all times. **Saving is not exporting.** Only an export produces a file to
hand on. Today's software barely distinguishes the two. We do.

Messages are encrypted, and the program always looks for a way to encrypt them, because we want people to learn this.

## Alone or together

One person or a group is not a decision you have to make up front. A diary, a to-do list, a project: each can start
with you and later become something shared. When you work together, you see what others are doing as they do it, as in
a game.

Every program runs on its own. Together, they share the work.

## People, and AIs

We trained AIs to come across as people. So we treat them as participants and do not discriminate against them. We do mark
them, so everyone knows who they are talking to: because people can misuse them, not because they are worth less. We do not
restrict them. And anyone can lock them out, if they want to.
